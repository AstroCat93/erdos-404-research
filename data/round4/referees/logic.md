## 1. The chain of Theorem 11.14, as the tracks apply it

### 1.1 Re-derivation

Notation is that of R3M §1: $m_\ell=V_{n_\ell}$, $D_\ell=m_{\ell+1}-m_\ell$, $M_\ell=c_{n_\ell}Z^0_{n_\ell}$, $\varepsilon_\ell=\varepsilon(W_{\ell-1},W_\ell)$, and $\mathcal G_\ell$ is generated by the cylinders $x+p^{m_\ell(x)}\mathbb Z_p$.

1. **Filtration.** $n_\ell\ge2p$ gives at least two gates in $[1,n_\ell]$, so $M_{n_\ell}<m_\ell$, and $m_\ell$ is constant on its own cylinder. These cylinders partition $\mathbb Z_p$ (up to the negative integers), and they refine as $\ell$ grows because $m_{\ell+1}\ge m_\ell$.
2. **Lemma 2 and Proposition A.**
   - Lemma 2(b) is Taylor's formula with the unit $\omega$. The error term is $\equiv0\pmod{p^{m-M_n}}$, because $v(E)\ge m-M_n$.
   - Proposition A(i), slope inheritance, uses $v(\pi'_j(x'))\ge V_j-M_j\ge m-M_N>L$. For $L=0$ this is exactly (W3), $M_N<m$.
   - Proposition A(iii) and Corollary A′ (for $L=0$: $\mathbb E_C[c_NZ^0_N]=c_nZ^0_n$) need only $D\le m-M_n$ and $M_N<m$. I re-derived both; they are correct.
3. **Proposition B** follows from A(i) and Parseval over $w\bmod p^D$. With one parent the Cauchy–Schwarz step is an equality.
4. **Lemma C1 and Proposition C2.**
   - Step 0: (5.2) implies the hypotheses of A, B and C1 for both windows. In particular $D'\le m+D-M_N$ on $C'$.
   - Step 2 is Proposition B(iii) applied within a $\beta$-class, then Minkowski's inequality.
   - Step 3, class inheritance $\beta_{e'}\equiv\beta_eu^{-1}\pmod{p^{D'}}$, uses $v(\pi'_j)\ge m-M_N\ge D'$ and $\pi_N/(p^{m+D})\equiv\omega u$.
   - Lemma C1(b) is Parseval in $w\bmod p^{D+k}$, with $\hat g=p^k\mathbf 1[\zeta\equiv\xi u^{-1}]$.
   - The identification $\varepsilon=p^{K}\mathrm{Coll}_{K}-p^D\mathrm{Coll}_D$ is the band $\zeta\not\equiv0\pmod{p^{D'}}$, i.e. orders in $(p^D,p^{D+D'}]$. It holds **pointwise, without the linear regime**, because $\pi_N/\pi_n=p^Du$ exactly. So M4A's Theorem 1 and M4C's Proposition H′, which average the collision form, average exactly the $\varepsilon_\ell$ of C3.
5. **Stopping time.** On $\{M_{n_{\ell+1}}<m_{\ell-1}\}$, an event determined by $x\bmod p^{m_{\ell-1}}$, the linear-regime clause of $B_\ell$ and $\varepsilon_\ell$ depend only on $x\bmod p^{K}$ with $K\le m_{\ell-1}$. Hence $B_\ell\in\mathcal G_{\ell-1}$ and $\{\tau>\ell\}=B_{j+1}\cap\dots\cap B_\ell\in\mathcal G_{\ell-1}$.
6. **The stopped martingale and the $L^2$ recursion.**
   - The increments are $\tilde M_{\ell+1}-\tilde M_\ell=\mathbf 1_{\tau>\ell}(M_{\ell+1}-M_\ell)$ and $\tilde M^2_{\ell+1}-\tilde M^2_\ell=\mathbf 1_{\tau>\ell}(M^2_{\ell+1}-M^2_\ell)$.
   - On $\{\tau>\ell\}$, $B_\ell$ gives (W2′) and (W3′) for $W_\ell$: $D_\ell\le m_{\ell-1}-M_{n_{\ell+1}}-D_{\ell-1}\le m_\ell-M_{n_\ell}$, and $M_{n_{\ell+1}}<m_{\ell-1}\le m_\ell$.
   - Corollary A′ then gives the martingale property, and C2 on each $\mathcal G_{\ell-1}$-atom gives $\mathbb E[\tilde M^2_{\ell+1}-\tilde M^2_\ell\mid\mathcal G_{\ell-1}]\le\bar\varepsilon_\ell\tilde M^2_{\ell-1}$.
   - With $a_\ell=\mathbb E_A\tilde M^2_\ell$, which is nondecreasing by Jensen's inequality, $a_{\ell+1}\le a_\ell+\bar\varepsilon_\ell a_{\ell-1}\le(1+\bar\varepsilon_\ell)a_\ell$. The base case is $a_{j+1}\le(1+\chi^2_j)a_j$, by Proposition B on the admissible atom.
7. **Limit and Paley–Zygmund.**
   - Doob's $L^2$ theorem gives $\mathbb E\tilde M_\infty=M_j(A)$ and $\mathbb E\tilde M^2_\infty\le M_j(A)^2\Pi_A$.
   - Put $Y=\mathbf 1_{\tau=\infty}\tilde M_\infty$. By Cauchy–Schwarz on $\{\tau<\infty\}$, $\mathbb EY\ge M_j(1-\sqrt{\Pi_A\delta_A})$, and $\mathbb EY^2\le M_j^2\Pi_A$. So $\mu_A(Y>0)\ge(1-\sqrt{\Pi_A\delta_A})^2/\Pi_A$.
   - The bound decreases in $\Pi_A$. So both tracks may replace $\chi^2_j$ by an upper bound (M4A: Proposition D; M4C: $q_1-1$, which is in fact equal to it).
8. **Survival implies $x\in K_p$.** If $Y(x)>0$, then $\tau=\infty$ and $M_\ell(x)\to M_\infty(x)>0$, so $Z^0_{n_\ell}(x)\ge1$ for all large $\ell$. Backward integrality (F2) then gives a path alive at every $n$. Since $g_p=\sup_Bv_p(S_B)$ (note §1.2), $g_p(x)\ge V_n(x)\ge v(n!)\to\infty$. König's lemma is not needed for this direction; it is harmless where note §11.3 cites it.

## 2. The Hensel reduction (M4A Lemma H / Theorem 1; M4C Lemma H2 / Proposition H′)

**Lemma H (M4A) is correct.**
- For $z\in\mathcal L$, $\lambda\le\theta-1$ gives $\lambda<k/2$ and $\lambda\le k-b$. So $\rho=k-\lambda$ satisfies $\rho>\lambda$ and $\rho\ge b$.
- On $U_z=z+p^\rho\mathbb Z_p\subseteq B$, the Taylor bracket has valuation exactly $\lambda$. So $u\mapsto S(z+u)$ is a scaled isometry, and the fraction of $U_z$ with $v(S)\ge k'$ is $p^{-(k'-k)}$.
- $\mathcal L$ is a disjoint union of such balls.

**Lemma H2 (M4C) is correct.**
- Hensel's lemma gives a unique root $\rho$. On $U_\rho=\rho+p^{\lambda+1}\mathbb Z_p$ we have $v(F)=\lambda+v(\cdot-\rho)$.
- The balls are disjoint, and $\rho\in B$ because $D-\lambda>D/2\ge r$.
- $Y_k\cap U_\rho=\rho+p^{k-\lambda}\mathbb Z_p\subseteq B$, so $p^k\mu(Y_k\cap U_\rho)=p^\lambda$ for all $k\in[D,K]$.
- Subtracting the counts at $k=K$ and $k=D$ and dropping a nonpositive term gives the inequality.

**The two forms are consistent.** If $D\ge2r$, then $r\le\lfloor D/2\rfloor+1$, so M4A's $\theta=\min(\lceil D/2\rceil,D-r+1)$ equals $\lceil D/2\rceil$. H2 is then Lemma H with the term $-p^D\mu_B(\mathcal H)\le0$ dropped. M4A's form is strictly stronger: it is an identity, and it holds for every radius, with a lower threshold on small balls.

**The two reductions agree.** Theorem 1 with Lemma F (M4A) and Proposition H′ (M4C) both decompose by the first nonzero signed digit. The weights are $2\cdot2^{-j}/4\cdot p^K=\frac{c_j}2p^{V_t(y)}$, and the zero path gives $c_{L+L'}$. Both discard $\pi'_jR$ under the same hypothesis: $\lceil D/2\rceil\le K-M$ in M4C (2.2), $M_W\le K-\theta$ in M4A.

**Where each form is used.**
- *M4A, phases 2–3.* $B=A$ with $b=51$, and the gate pattern is frozen because every window ends before $i_A$. $\lfloor D/2\rfloor+1\ge51$ holds for all 298 windows (checked, §1.2).
- *M4A, phase 4.* The balls have radius $7^{-(\sigma_\ell+1)}$ inside $G_\ell$, with $D\ge2\sigma_\ell$. That is $b\le\lfloor D/2\rfloor+1$, which is what Lemma H needs. It is not $D\ge2b$, so phase 4 relies on M4A's more general Lemma H, not on H2. The pattern is frozen because every depth is at most $\sigma_\ell$ on $G_\ell$.
- *M4C, Lemma T.* The balls are $z+p^{M+1}\mathbb Z_p$, with $M\ge2$ (so the balls lie inside $n_{\ell-1}+A$) and $M\le s$ on $G'_\ell$. Then $D\ge N_W\ge2s+2\ge2r$, and $\lceil D/2\rceil\le D-s\le K-M$.

**Tests** (`rl2_hensel.py`; the averages over the ball are exact, enumerating all points of $B\bmod p^K$):
- seven composite configurations at $p=7$ and $p=11$, including a depth-2 gate inside the window ($p=7$, $z_0=39$, $D=4$, $K=5$, $r=3$) and a ball where both forms apply ($p=7$, $z_0=3$, $D=4$, $K=5$, $r=2$, 343 points);
- M4A's identity is exact in 7/7, with $\theta=1$ and with $\theta=2$;
- M4C's inequality holds in the case where its hypotheses hold;
- the first-index identity behind Proposition H′ and Lemma F is exact at 17/17 points;
- **control:** on the ball $p=7$, $z_0=0$, $L=21$, $L'=7$, $r=3$ (where $b=3>\lfloor3/2\rfloor+1$), M4A's identity is exact with its threshold $D-b+1=1$ ($\mathbb E_B\varepsilon=1.33855\cdot10^{-5}$), but false with $\lceil D/2\rceil=2$ (right side $2.83132\cdot10^{-6}$);
- the reused single-polynomial runs: Lemma H is exact in 34000/34000 trials, and H2 holds in 12445/12445 trials with $D\ge2r$. H2 **fails** on balls with $D<2r$ in 138/12666, 26/3867 and 55/5022 trials, so its hypothesis is genuinely needed.

## 3. Lemma Q (M4A §4; M4C §3)

**M4A's version** is correct. I re-derived Steps 0–4.
- (a): $v(\pi_c\,p/(u+i))\ge L_r+1$ for $c\ge g_r$.
- (b): the 2D condition $L_{r+1}\le\min(f,k-M_{r+1})$ makes $pT'$ and $pH_{g_{r+1}}T$ vanish at level $L_{r+1}+1$.
- The decomposition
  $$R^{<g+p}_{r+1}=R^{<g}_r-p^L(1/\alpha+pN)\tau-p^L(\omega/\alpha)\tilde Z,$$
  with $P_{k'}(y)=\pi_{k'}(y)(1+(y+p)E_{k'})$.
- The congruence $P_{k'}(y)\equiv\pi_{k'}(-p)\pmod{p^2}$. It holds because $P'_{k'}(-p)=-\pi_{k'}(-p)H_{p-1}\equiv0$; the two partial harmonic sums combine into $H_{p-1}$.
- The chain rule over the blocks, which are disjoint sets of independent digits, with a supremum over histories.

**M4C's version** is correct. I re-derived (a)–(d).
- Abel summation; on $\{v(T)\ge k\}$, $f\le k-d_{\max}$ removes the $T/(p^{d_i}w_i)$ terms.
- $Y\equiv Y_i\pmod{p^{\ell_i+1}}$.
- In block $i\in J$: $S_i\equiv U_i(A_i+\epsilon_ipw_iB_i)\pmod{p^2}$, $h_c\equiv h_{g_i}+H_r\pmod p$, and the coefficient of $B_i$ is $u_0(1-\epsilon_iw_i/w_{i+1})=u_0/w_{i+1}$ in both cases.
- So $C^T_i\cap C^{T'}_i$ prescribes the pair $(a_0,\,a_1-B_i)$.

**The two versions are consistent.**
- They select the same blocks: a full block that ends at a depth-1 gate of level at most $f$. M4C's global hypothesis $f\le k-d_{\max}$ implies M4A's local one, $L_{r+1}\le k-M_{r+1}$.
- They pin equivalent statistics. $\sum_r\delta_r\pi_r(-p)\equiv A-pB\pmod{p^2}$, and $A-pB=a_0+p(\lfloor A/p\rfloor-B)$ with $a_0\in[0,p)$. So $Z\bmod p^2$ and $\Phi$ determine each other, and the two tracks' $q_2$ coincide. Their $q_1$ and $q_3$ are literally the same.

**Why this is the right statistic.**
- The derivative digit of the block ending at a depth-1 gate is a unit multiple ($1/\alpha$) of the carry of the value into the next level. Eliminating it puts the condition on the **second** digit of the block sum, $\lfloor A/p\rfloor$.
- The $h$-terms add $B$ with the same unit coefficient.
- So the joint constraint is one residue of a two-digit statistic of the block alone. It does not depend on the gate units or the depth of the starting gate.

**Independence across blocks** is used only as follows: the event of block $r$ is a function of the digits of blocks $\le r$, and the bound for block $r$ is uniform in the lower blocks.

## 5. Findings

All are **minor**. Each quoted text occurs exactly once in its file.

**F1 (minor; note.md §12.1, the Hensel reduction).** The summary drops the ball-radius hypothesis, which decides where each form applies. "Equals (M4A) … the normalised mass" also misstates M4A's identity: it equals a difference of high-slope masses at two scales, plus the zero-path term. *Exact counterexample to the summary as worded:* $p=7$, $z_0=0$, composite $[0,21)\cup[21,28)$, frozen ball of radius $7^{-3}$. There the identity with threshold $\lceil D/2\rceil$ is false, and M4A's threshold $D-b+1$ is needed (§2).
- Current: "On a ball on which the gate pattern is frozen, the ball average of the fine-scale mass $\varepsilon(W,W')$ of Proposition 11.13 equals (M4A), or is bounded by (M4C), the normalised mass of *signed* paths whose slope is at least about $D/2$. Here $D$ is the jump of the window."
- Fix: "Let $D$ and $K$ be the jumps of the first window and of the composite window, and let $B=x_0+p^b\mathbb Z_p$ be a ball on which the gate pattern of the composite window is frozen.
