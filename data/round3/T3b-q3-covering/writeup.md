## 1. Exact self-similarity for windows of length $p^D$ (idea 1, first half)

### 1.1 Statement

**Theorem S1 [proved].** Let $p$ be a prime, $1\le D<t$, $H=p^D$, $X=p^tK'$ with $K'\in\mathbb Z_p$ (unit or not). For $0\le i<H$ put $\ell(i)=\lfloor\log_pi\rfloor$ ($\ell(0)=0$). Then
$$y_i(K')=\prod_{s=1}^{i}\big(X-(p^D-s)\big)=i!\cdot A_i\cdot B_i(K'),\qquad A_i=(-1)^i\binom{p^D-1}{i}=\prod_{s=1}^{i}\Big(1-\frac{p^D}{s}\Big),\qquad B_i(K')=\prod_{s=1}^{i}\Big(1+\frac{p^tK'}{s-p^D}\Big).$$
Here $A_i\in\mathbb Z$ does not depend on $t$ or $K'$, and $B_i(K')\in\mathbb Z_p$. Moreover:

- (a) $A_i\equiv1\pmod{p^{\,D-\ell(i)}}$;
- (b) $B_i(K')\equiv1\pmod{p^{\,t+v(K')-\ell(i)}}$;
- (c) $A_i\equiv1-p^DH_i\pmod{p^{\,2(D-\ell(i))}}$, where $H_i=\sum_{s\le i}1/s$;
- (d) $v\big(y_i(K')-i!A_i\big)\ \ge\ t+v(K')+v(i!)-\ell(i)$.

Equivalently, in binomial form, $y_i(K')=i!\binom{p^tK'-p^D+i}{i}$ (a generalised binomial coefficient with $p$-adic upper argument), and (a)–(d) describe its digit structure.

*Proof.* $X-(p^D-s)=(s-p^D)\big(1+X/(s-p^D)\big)$ for $1\le s\le i<p^D$, so $y_i=\prod_{s\le i}(s-p^D)\cdot B_i$. Next $\prod_{s\le i}(s-p^D)=(-1)^i\prod_{s\le i}(p^D-s)=(-1)^i\frac{(p^D-1)!}{(p^D-1-i)!}=(-1)^i\,i!\binom{p^D-1}{i}$, and also $=i!\prod_{s\le i}(1-p^D/s)$. This gives the identity and the two forms of $A_i$.

- (a) For $s\le i<p^D$ we have $v(s)\le\ell(i)<D$, so $1-p^D/s\in1+p^{D-v(s)}\mathbb Z_p\subseteq1+p^{D-\ell(i)}\mathbb Z_p$, which is a multiplicative group.
- (b) $v\big(p^tK'/(s-p^D)\big)=t+v(K')-v(s-p^D)=t+v(K')-v(s)\ge t+v(K')-\ell(i)$, since $v(s)<D$ gives $v(s-p^D)=v(s)$. Again $1+p^m\mathbb Z_p$ is a group.
- (c) Put $a_s=p^D/s$, so $v(a_s)\ge m:=D-\ell(i)\ge1$. Expanding $\prod_{s\le i}(1-a_s)=1-\sum_sa_s+\sum_{s<s'}a_sa_{s'}-\cdots$, every term with at least two factors has valuation $\ge2m$. Hence $A_i\equiv1-p^DH_i\pmod{p^{2m}}$.
- (d) $y_i-i!A_i=i!A_i(B_i-1)$, $v(A_i)=0$ by (a), and (b). ∎

*Remarks.* (i) The first-order term $-p^DH_i$ in (c) is the "$N\sum(-1)^uH_u/u!$" of note §6 and Theorem 3.10 in the present normalisation (there the window was written from its right end). (ii) For general $H$ the same factorisation reads $y_i(K')=\pi_i(-H)\cdot\prod_{s\le i}\big(1+p^tK'/(s-H)\big)$ with $\pi_i(-H)=(-1)^i(H-1)!/(H-1-i)!$; this is note Lemma 10.11(a) with the explicit twist factor, and (b),(d) hold with $\ell(i)$ replaced by $\max_{s\le i}v(s-H)=\max_{s\le i}v(H-s)$. (iii) Nothing in S1 needs $K'$ to be a unit; for $v(K')>0$ the bounds improve by $v(K')$. This is used in §2.3: the depth-$t$ window at the unit class $K'$ is the depth-$(t-1)$ window at the non-unit class $pK'$, with one more digit of demand.

### 1.2 What S1 says about the window $H_t=p^{t-1}$

Take $D=t-1$ (the family of note Theorem 10.16). Then:

- the elements are $i!\cdot(\text{unit})$ with $v(y_i)=v(i!)$ (note Prop. 10.15(i)), and the unit $A_iB_i$ is $\equiv1\pmod{p^{\,t-1-\ell(i)}}$; so $y_i\equiv i!\pmod{p^{\,v(i!)+t-1-\ell(i)}}$, at least one digit beyond the leading one for every $i$;
- the position $K'$ enters only from absolute digit $t+v(i!)-\ell(i)$ on;
- the demand is $M=t+c$ with $c=v((p^{t-1}-1)!)=\frac{p^{t-1}-1}{p-1}-(t-1)$, i.e. $M=v\big((p^{t-1})!\big)+1$.

So $\mathrm{Cov}(p,t,p^{t-1})$ is a unit-perturbation (relative size $p^{-(t-1-\ell(i))}$, at worst $p^{-1}$ for $i\ge p^{t-2}$) of the statement

> ($\mathcal F_t$) the $2^{p^{t-1}}$ sums of distinct factorials $\sum_{i\in S}i!$, $S\subseteq[0,p^{t-1})$, cover $\mathbb Z/p^{\,v(p^{t-1}!)+1}$,

which is the achievement-set statement $A_0\bmod p^{v(N!)+1}=$ everything at $N=p^{t-1}$ (note §1.5). The perturbation is small in relative terms but, because the demand sits at digit $v(p^{t-1}!)+1$ while $y_i$ and $i!$ already differ at digit $v(i!)+t-1-\ell(i)\le c+1$ for the large $i$, it has full size at the digits that decide covering. This is the barrier of note Theorem 3.10 in explicit form: see §2.4 for the precise negative statement.

## 2. The inductive structure, and exactly what an induction must carry (idea 1, second half; idea 2)

### 2.1 Mini-block decomposition (note Prop. 10.15(ii), restated with gate levels)

Let $H=pH'\le p^t$, $X=p^tK'$, $M=t+c$. Write $i=pq+r$ with $0\le r<p$, $0\le q<H'$, and $m_q:=H'-q\in[1,H']$.

**Theorem S2 [proved; this is note Prop. 10.15(ii) plus bookkeeping].**
- (i) $y_{pq+r}(X)=p^q\;y'_q\;U_q(X)\;w_{q,r}(X)$, where $y'_q=\prod_{s'=1}^{q}\big(X/p-(H'-s')\big)$ is the depth-$(t-1)$ window element of length $H'$ at the position $X/p=p^{t-1}K'$, $U_q(X)=\prod_{s\le pq,\,p\nmid s}(X-pH'+s)$ is a unit with $U_q(X)\equiv U_q(0)\pmod{p^t}$, and $w_{q,r}(X)=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\big(X-pm_q+j\big)\equiv\omega_r(pm_q):=\prod_{j=1}^{r}(j-pm_q)\pmod{p^t}$.
- (ii) Block $q$ is $\{y_{pq+r}\}_{r<p}$; all its elements have valuation $e_q=q+v(y'_q)=v(y_{pq})$, and $e_{q+1}-e_q=1+v(H'-1-q)$ for $q<H'-1$. In the language of note §3.5, block $q$ is a gate of level $v_p(H'-1-q)$, and the last block ($q=H'-1$) is a gate of level $t-1$ with $e_{H'-1}=c$ and $M-c=t$.
- (iii) The demand grows by exactly $H'$ from depth $t-1$ (length $H'$) to depth $t$ (length $pH'$): $M=M'+H'$ with $M'=(t-1)+v((H'-1)!)$.
- (iv) The subset sums are $\Sigma_H=\big\{\sum_{q<H'}y'_q\cdot p^qU_q(X)\,w_{q,S_q}(X):\ S_q\subseteq[0,p)\big\}$, $w_{q,S}:=\sum_{r\in S}w_{q,r}$. For the last block, $m_{H'-1}=1$ and $\{\omega_S(p)\}_S=V_p:=(p-1)!\cdot\Sigma(E_p)$, the universal gate set of note Theorem 3.9(b) / Cor. 10.13(a).

*Proof.* (i) Among the factors $X-pH'+s$, $1\le s\le pq$, those with $s=ps'$ give $p^q\prod_{s'\le q}(X/p-H'+s')=p^qy'_q$; the others form $U_q(X)$, each factor $\equiv s\not\equiv0\pmod p$, and $X\equiv0\pmod{p^t}$ gives $U_q(X)\equiv U_q(0)$. The remaining factors $s=pq+j$, $1\le j\le r$, give $w_{q,r}$. (ii) $v(w_{q,r})=0$ since $w_{q,r}\equiv r!\pmod p$; $v(y'_{q+1}/y'_q)=v(X/p-(H'-q-1))=v(H'-1-q)$ because $0<H'-1-q<p^{t-1}$. The last block: $v(y_{p(H'-1)})=v((pH'-1)!)-v((p-1)!)=c$. (iii) $c=v((pH'-1)!)=(H'-1)+v((H'-1)!)$ by Legendre. (iv) is (i) summed; for $m=1$, $\prod_{j\le r}(j-p)=(-1)^r(p-1)!/(p-1-r)!=(p-1)!(-1)^u/u!$ with $u=p-1-r$ ($p$ odd). ∎

So "the depth-$t$ window of length $pH'$" is "the depth-$(t-1)$ window of length $H'$ with each element $y'_q$ replaced by the $p$-block $y'_q\cdot p^qU_q\cdot\{w_{q,r}\}_{r<p}$". Each block contributes one extra digit of demand and $p-1$ extra elements; a block of level $<L_p$ covers its own extra digit from any carry (note Theorem 3.9(c)); the blocks of level $\ge L_p$ — in particular the final one, of level $t-1$ — do not. This is the precise sense in which the induction on $t$ gains nothing for free: the hard part at depth $t$ (the level-$(t-1)$ gate at the end) is new at depth $t$.

### 2.2 The exact lifting criterion at the final gate

**Theorem S3 (exact fibre criterion) [proved].** In the setting of S2, let $R\subseteq\mathbb Z/p^M$ be the set of subset sums of the first $p(H'-1)$ elements (the window of length $H-p$), let $u:=y_{p(H'-1)}/p^c$ (a $p$-adic unit), and for $T\in\mathbb Z/p^c$ let
$$B_T:=\big\{(r-T)/p^c\bmod p^t:\ r\in R,\ r\equiv T\pmod{p^c}\big\}\subseteq\mathbb Z/p^t .$$
Then the window of length $H$ covers $\mathbb Z/p^M$ iff for **every** $T\in\mathbb Z/p^c$: $\;B_T+u\,V_p=\mathbb Z/p^t$ (with $V_p$ reduced mod $p^t$).

*Proof.* By S2(iv) with $q=H'-1$: $\Sigma_H=R+y_{p(H'-1)}\{w_{H'-1,S}\}_S$ and $y_{p(H'-1)}w_{H'-1,S}\equiv p^cu\,\omega_S(p)\pmod{p^{c+t}}$, since $w\equiv\omega_S(p)\pmod{p^t}$. A target $z\equiv T\pmod{p^c}$ is a sum iff $r+p^cu\omega_S(p)\equiv z\pmod{p^M}$ for some $r\in R$, $S$; then $r\equiv T\pmod{p^c}$ and, dividing by $p^c$, $(r-T)/p^c+u\omega_S(p)\equiv(z-T)/p^c\pmod{p^t}$. As $z$ runs over the class of $T$, $(z-T)/p^c$ runs over $\mathbb Z/p^t$. ∎

This is precisely the fibre decomposition at level $k=c$ that programs A, B, C (note §10.2) and A′, C′ (§4) implement, with the high elements being exactly the last block. *Independent exact check [computer-assisted, 2026-10-21]:* `src/s3_check.py` (exact integers, no shared code with the programs) evaluates, for 18 configurations $(p,t,H=pH')$ with $p\in\{3,5,7,11,13\}$, $t\le4$, $H\le33$ and every unit class $K'\bmod p^c$ (466 classes, 107 of them not covered), (1) direct covering of $\mathbb Z/p^M$ by the $2^H$ subset sums, (2) the criterion of Theorem S3 fibre by fibre, and (3) the identity $\{w_{H'-1,S}\bmod p^t\}_S=V_p\bmod p^t$ of S2(iv); on every class the direct miss count equals the sum of the fibre miss counts and (3) holds (`logs/s3_check.log`, 13 s). The theorem isolates what the *induction* has to know about the preceding window:

**Corollary S3′ (what must be carried) [proved].**
- (i) Only the family of fibres $(B_T)_{T\bmod p^c}$ of the carries enters. Any other information about $R$ (multiplicities, which subsets realise which carry, the lower window structure) is irrelevant to $\mathrm{Cov}(p,t,pH')$.
- (ii) *Fullness never suffices.* Suppose only that each $B_T$ meets every class mod $p^d$ for some $d$ with $D_p\le d<t$. Then there are sets $B_T$ with this property for which $B_T+uV_p\ne\mathbb Z/p^t$. (By the abstract core of note Theorem 10.12 — "$T\subseteq A+B$ for every $A$ meeting all cosets of a subgroup $K$ iff $B$ contains a coset of $K$", with $G=\mathbb Z/p^t$, $K=p^d\mathbb Z/p^t$, $B=uV_p$, $T=G$ — the conclusion holds for every such $B_T$ iff $uV_p\bmod p^t$ contains a complete coset $c_0+p^d\mathbb Z/p^t$. The values of $V_p$ are integers in an interval of length $<e(p-1)!\le p^{D_p}\le p^d$, hence pairwise incongruent mod $p^d$; a coset of $p^d$ in $\mathbb Z/p^t$ has $p^{t-d}\ge p$ elements all congruent mod $p^d$. Contradiction.) This is note Cor. 10.13(a) in the fibre normalisation.
- (iii) *A cardinality condition that does suffice:* if $|B_T|>p^t-|uV_p\bmod p^t|$ for every $T$, then $\mathrm{Cov}(p,t,pH')$ holds (the complement of $B_T$ is too small to contain a translate of $-uV_p$). Since $|V_p|\le2^p$ this asks the carries to miss fewer than $2^p$ residues per fibre — essentially full fibres; it is not a usable induction hypothesis.
- (iv) *The Fourier form* (Theorem S4 below) is the only one of these that degrades gracefully.

*Proof of (i),(iii).* Immediate from S3. ∎

### 2.3 The Fourier form of the lifting criterion

**Theorem S4 (Fourier lifting across the final gate) [proved].** Let $\mu_T$ be any probability measure on $\mathbb Z/p^t$ with support contained in $B_T$ (for instance the path-counting measure: the number of subsets of the first $p(H'-1)$ elements producing each carry, normalised). Put $\hat\mu_T(\eta)=\sum_b\mu_T(b)e(\eta b/p^t)$ and
$$\hat E_p(\theta):=\prod_{u=0}^{p-1}\Big|\cos\Big(\pi\theta\,\frac{(p-1)!(-1)^u}{u!}\Big)\Big|\qquad(\theta\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z),$$
the absolute Fourier transform of the uniform measure on the $2^p$ subset sums $V_p$. If
$$\sum_{0\ne\eta\in\mathbb Z/p^t}|\hat\mu_T(\eta)|\;\hat E_p\big(u\eta/p^t\big)<1,$$
then $B_T+uV_p=\mathbb Z/p^t$. Consequently, if this holds for every $T\bmod p^c$, then $\mathrm{Cov}(p,t,pH')$ holds.

*Proof.* Let $\lambda$ be the uniform measure on the multiset $\{u\omega_S(p):S\subseteq[0,p)\}$, so $\hat\lambda(\eta)=\prod_u\frac{1+e(\eta u\omega_u/p^t)}2$ and $|\hat\lambda(\eta)|=\hat E_p(u\eta/p^t)$. The convolution $\mu_T*\lambda$ is supported on $B_T+uV_p$, and by Fourier inversion on $\mathbb Z/p^t$, $(\mu_T*\lambda)(z)=p^{-t}\sum_\eta\hat\mu_T(\eta)\hat\lambda(\eta)e(-\eta z/p^t)\ge p^{-t}\big(1-\sum_{\eta\ne0}|\hat\mu_T(\eta)||\hat\lambda(\eta)|\big)>0$ for every $z$. ∎

*Discussion.* $\hat E_p$ is **universal**: it depends on $p$ only, not on $t$, $H'$ or $K'$. The entire depth dependence of $\mathrm{Cov}(p,t,pH')$ is in $\hat\mu_T$, the Fourier profile of the carry fibre. Group the frequencies by exact order: $\eta=p^{t-d}\eta'$ with $\eta'\in(\mathbb Z/p^d)^\times$ sees $\hat E_p(u\eta'/p^d)$, so the criterion reads
$$\sum_{d=1}^{t}\ \sum_{\eta'\in(\mathbb Z/p^d)^\times}\big|\hat\mu_T(p^{t-d}\eta')\big|\;\hat E_p(u\eta'/p^d)<1 .$$

### 2.4 Why there is no induction from the factorials, in one line [proved]

Let $z_i=i!\,u_i$ with $u_i\in1+p^{a_i}\mathbb Z_p$ and let $m$ be a target modulus. Then $\sum_{i\in S}z_i\equiv\sum_{i\in S}i!\pmod{p^{\min_{i\in S}(v(i!)+a_i)}}$. So covering mod $p^m$ by the $z_i$ follows from covering mod $p^m$ by the factorials only if the latter can be done with subsets $S\subseteq I_m:=\{i:v(i!)+a_i\ge m\}$. For the window $H=p^{t-1}$, $a_i=t-1-\ell(i)$ (S1(a)) and $m=t+c$ with $c=v((p^{t-1}-1)!)\ge v(i!)$ for all $i<p^{t-1}$, so $v(i!)+a_i\ge t+c$ would need $v(i!)\ge c+1+\ell(i)>c$: $I_m=\emptyset$. The same holds with $a_i$ replaced by $t+v(i!)-\ell(i)-v(i!)=t-\ell(i)$ (the position-dependent part, S1(b)). Hence **no element of the window is congruent to its factorial at the top digit of the demand**, and the only usable relation between $\mathrm{Cov}(p,t,p^{t-1})$ and ($\mathcal F_t$) is through the full distribution of the top digits — Theorems S3/S4 — not through congruences. (This is the content of note Theorem 3.10 for this family, made explicit.)

### 2.5 Windows with $p\mid H$ and per-position covering (idea 2)

Nothing in this subsection is deep; it records what is provable and what is not.

- **Depth shift [proved].** For $H\le p^{t-1}$, the depth-$t$ window at the unit class $K'$ is the depth-$(t-1)$ window at the non-unit class $pK'$: $y^{(t)}_i(K')=\prod_s(p^{t-1}\cdot pK'-(H-s))=y^{(t-1)}_i(pK')$, with the demand raised by one digit ($M=M'+1$). Hence the family $\{\mathrm{Cov}(p,t,H)\}_{t\ge t_0}$ is one statement about the one-parameter family of windows $\prod_{s\le i}(Y-(H-s))$ as $Y\to0$ in $p^{t_0}\mathbb Z_p$: the demand $t_0+v(Y/p^{t_0})+c$ grows while the elements converge to the integers $\pi_i(-H)$. Together with the integrality of $\pi_i(-H)$ this gives the $K'$-free necessary condition of note Lemma 10.11(c) at $k=t$: $\mathrm{Cov}(p,t,H)$ requires the integer subset sums of $\{\pi_i(-H)\}_{i<H}$ to cover $\mathbb Z/p^t$, hence $p^t\le2e(H-1)!+1$.
- **Chains with $p\mid H$ [proved, from note Prop. 10.14; corrected 2026-10-20].** Note Prop. 10.14 gives $\mathrm{Cov}(p,t,H)\Rightarrow\mathrm{Cov}(p,t,H+1)$ when $p\nmid H$ (and $H+1\le p^t$), and $\mathrm{Cov}(p,t,H)\Rightarrow\mathrm{Cov}(p,t,H+p)$ when $1\le v_p(H)\le L_p$ (and $H+p<p^t$). Step (a) is not available at a multiple of $p$, so from a certified $H_0$ the reach is: $H_0,H_0+1,\dots$ up to the first multiple $pm_0\ge H_0$ of $p$, and then **only the multiples** $pm$, $m_0\le m\le p^{L_p}$ (the (b) step from $pm$ needs $v_p(pm)=1+v_p(m)\le L_p$, so the chain passes $p^{L_p}$ and stops at $p^{L_p+1}$). *The 2026-10-12 version of this paragraph claimed every $H$ in $[H_0,p^{L_p+1}]$; that is false in general: `src/chain_monotone_check.py` (exact DP over all unit classes, `logs/chain_monotone_check.log`) shows e.g. $\mathrm{Cov}(13,3,19)$ holds, $H=20,\dots,26$ hold (reached), $H=27,\dots,31$ **fail** (not reached), $H=32$ holds; $\mathrm{Cov}(7,3,19)$ holds, $20,21$ hold, $22,23,24$ **fail**, $28$ holds (reached); $\mathrm{Cov}(13,2,10)$ holds, $11,12,13$ hold, $14,\dots,18$ **fail**, $19$ holds — exactly the non-monotonicity recorded in note §10.2.* What is correctly implied: at $p=13$ ($L_{13}=2$) from the certified uniform $\mathrm{Cov}(13,7,39)$ ($39=3\cdot13$): $\mathrm{Cov}(13,7,13m)$ for $3\le m\le169$, in particular $H=13^2$ and $H=13^3$; at $p=7$ ($L_7=2$) from $\mathrm{Cov}(7,6,34)$: $H=34,35$ and $7m$ for $5\le m\le49$, including $49$ and $343$; at $p=11$ ($L_{11}=2$) from $\mathrm{Cov}(11,6,33)$: $11m$ for $3\le m\le121$, including $121$ and $1331$. These are consequences of note Prop. 10.14 only; they are recorded because the $H=p^D$ windows are the ones S1 describes, and because they show that **at the certified depths the length-$p^D$ family ($D\le3$) is no harder than the minimal family**. The chains stop at $p^{L_p+1}$, and $\mathrm{Cov}(p,t,p^{L_p+1}+p)$ is not decided by them.
- **Per-position accounting [proved; from note Theorem 3.12(a)].** A position certificate at $N^*=p^tK''$ with window $H$ certifies the whole class $K''\bmod p^c$. To exclude every first failure of depth $t$ below $p^D$ one needs $\min\big(p^{D-t},p^c\big)(1-1/p)$ distinct certificates. At $p=19$, $t=7$, $H=45$: $c=2$, so $D=8$ needs the 18 positions $K''\le18$ (done, note §10.2) but $D=9$ needs **all 342 classes mod $19^2$**. At $p=23$, $t=7$, $H=43$: $c=1$, so the 22 positions of note §10.2 already certify all depth-7 failures below $23^D$ for every $D$. This asymmetry is why $p=19$ depth 8 is the natural next computation (§4), while at $p=23$ depth 8 would need $23^8$-bit fibres (9.8 GB), beyond the memory cap.
- **What is not provable here.** No structural theorem makes per-position covering (UC′) easier than uniform covering: Theorem S3 applies verbatim to a single position (with $B_T$ the fibre at that position), and the only position-specific structure is through $u$ and $B_T$.

## 3. The $L^2$ question (idea 3)

### 3.1 Set-up

Let $Y=(y_1,\dots,y_n)$ be elements of $\mathbb Z/p^m$, $N(r)=\#\{S\subseteq[n]:\sum_Sy_i\equiv r\}$, $\bar N=2^n/p^m$, and $\hat\nu(\xi)=2^{-n}\sum_rN(r)e(\xi r/p^m)=\prod_i\frac{1+e(\xi y_i/p^m)}2$, so $|\hat\nu(\xi)|=\prod_i|\cos(\pi\xi y_i/p^m)|$. Define
$$L^1(Y)=\sum_{\xi\ne0}|\hat\nu(\xi)|,\qquad L^2(Y)=p^m\sum_{\xi\ne0}|\hat\nu(\xi)|^2=\sum_r\Big(\frac{N(r)}{\bar N}-1\Big)^2$$
(the second equality is Parseval).

### 3.2 The criteria and their comparison

**Theorem S5 [proved].**
- (a) *(Chebyshev)* $\#\{r:N(r)=0\}\le\#\{r:|N(r)-\bar N|\ge\bar N\}\le L^2(Y)$. Hence $L^2(Y)<1$ implies that every residue is a subset sum. (Note Prop. 3.13(b) states the fraction form $\le p^{-m}L^2$.)
- (b) *(Comparison)* $L^1(Y)\le\sqrt{(p^m-1)\sum_{\xi\ne0}|\hat\nu|^2}<\sqrt{L^2(Y)}$. So $L^2(Y)<1\Rightarrow L^1(Y)<1$: **the second-moment criterion is never weaker than the $L^1$ criterion; it has strictly less room.**
- (c) *(Lower bound at depth)* Let $Y$ be a window $y_i(K')$, $i<H$, of $\mathrm{Cov}(p,t,H)$ and $\rho=(H-1)\bmod p$. Then
$$L^2(Y)\ \ge\ c_1(p,\rho)\,p^{M},\qquad c_1(p,\rho):=\sum_{\xi'=1}^{p-1}\ \prod_{u=0}^{\rho}\cos^2\Big(\pi\xi'\,\frac{(-1)^\rho\rho!\,(-1)^u/u!}{p}\Big)>0,$$
a constant independent of $t$, $c$, $K'$ and of $H$ beyond $\rho$. Consequently the Chebyshev criterion can certify $\mathrm{Cov}(p,t,H)$ only if $p^{t+c}<1/c_1(p,\rho)$: for each $p$ only finitely many $(t,c)$.
- (d) *(Random model)* With each $|\cos|$ replaced by its mean over a uniform phase ($\mathbb E|\cos|=2/\pi$, $\mathbb E\cos^2=\tfrac12$) and $n_d=\#\{i:v(y_i)<d\}$: $L^1_{\rm rm}=\sum_{d=1}^{m}(p^d-p^{d-1})(2/\pi)^{n_d}$ (the budget of note Theorem 10.16) and $L^2_{\rm rm}=p^m\sum_{d=1}^{m}(p^d-p^{d-1})2^{-n_d}$. Along any family with $m\to\infty$ and $n_1$ bounded (every window: $n_1=\rho+1\le p$) the $d=1$ term alone, $p^m(p-1)2^{-n_1}$, diverges.
- (e) *(Fibre hybrid; corrected 2026-10-20)* Fix a level $k$, let $B_T\subseteq\mathbb Z/F$ ($F=p^{m-k}$) be the fibre set of the low subset sums over $T\bmod p^k$, $\hat B_T$ the Fourier transform of the uniform measure on $B_T$, and $\nu_U$ the uniform subset-sum measure of the high elements divided by $p^k$. Then [proved]
$$\#\{\text{residues missed in fibre }T\}\ \le\ \mathrm{Cheb}_T:=F\sum_{\eta\ne0}|\hat B_T(\eta)|^2|\hat\nu_U(\eta)|^2\ \le\ \mathrm{CS}_T:=F\Big(\frac F{|B_T|}-1\Big)\sup_{\eta\ne0}|\hat\nu_U(\eta)|^2 .$$
  The quantity $\mathrm{Hyb}_T:=(F/|B_T|-1)\sum_{\eta\ne0}|\hat\nu_U(\eta)|^2$ is **not** an upper bound for $\mathrm{Cheb}_T$ in general (the 2026-10-12 text wrongly stated "fibre $T$ is covered if $\mathrm{Hyb}_T<1$"; the pointwise inequality $|\hat B_T(\eta)|^2\le1/|B_T|-1/F$ it would need is false, e.g. for $|B_T|=1$; `logs/l2_hybrid_fix.log` lists certified windows with $\mathrm{Hyb}_T<\mathrm{Cheb}_T$ in every fibre). $\mathrm{Hyb}_T$ is the expectation of $\mathrm{Cheb}_T$ when $B_T$ is replaced by a random set of the same size [heuristic], and in the random model it is $\approx(1-1/p)\,F/R^*_T$ with $R^*_T=|B_T|2^{n_U}/F$ the per-fibre expected multiplicity (the $R^*$ of note Conj. 10.24 with sets in place of multisets). So the hybrid criterion needs, heuristically, $R^*\gtrsim F=p^{m-k}$, whereas the observed threshold is $R^*\approx1.5\ln(np^m)$; and the only general rigorous relaxation, $\mathrm{CS}_T<1$, needs $\sup_{\eta\ne0}|\hat\nu_U|^2<|B_T|/(F(F-|B_T|))$, i.e. essentially full fibres, because the supremum is $\ge$ the value at the archimedean resonance $\eta=1$ (note Cor. 10.20), which tends to $1$ at depth.

*Proofs.* (a) Chebyshev with the identity $\sum_r(N(r)-\bar N)^2=\bar N^2L^2(Y)$. (b) Cauchy–Schwarz over the $p^m-1$ nonzero frequencies. (c) Keep only the $p-1$ frequencies $\xi=p^{m-1}\xi'$ of exact order $p$. For them $|\hat\nu(\xi)|=\prod_{i:v(y_i)=0}|\cos(\pi\xi'y_i/p)|$, because elements with $v(y_i)\ge1$ contribute $|\cos(\pi\cdot\text{integer})|=1$. By note Lemma 10.11(d) the units are $y_0,\dots,y_\rho$ with $y_i\equiv(-1)^\rho\rho!(-1)^u/u!\pmod p$, $u=\rho-i$, independently of $K'$. No factor vanishes: $\cos(\pi a/p)=0$ would need $a/p\equiv\tfrac12\pmod1$, impossible for odd $p$. So $c_1>0$ and $L^2(Y)\ge p^m\sum_{\xi'}|\hat\nu(p^{m-1}\xi')|^2=c_1p^m$ with $m=M$. (d) is a computation. (e) In fibre $T$ the number of pairs ($b\in B_T$, high subset $S$) with $b+s_S\equiv z\pmod F$ is $N_T(z)=(|B_T|2^{n_U}/F)\sum_\eta\hat B_T(\eta)\hat\nu_U(\eta)e(-\eta z/F)$; its mean over $z$ is $\bar N_T=|B_T|2^{n_U}/F$ (the $\eta=0$ term) and $\sum_z(N_T(z)/\bar N_T-1)^2=F\sum_{\eta\ne0}|\hat B_T|^2|\hat\nu_U|^2$ by Parseval on $\mathbb Z/F$. Chebyshev gives the count bound $\mathrm{Cheb}_T$. Parseval for the uniform measure on $B_T$ gives $\sum_{\eta\ne0}|\hat B_T|^2=F/|B_T|-1$, and bounding $|\hat\nu_U|^2$ by its supremum gives $\mathrm{CS}_T$. The random-set evaluation uses $\mathbb E|\hat B_T(\eta)|^2=(F-|B_T|)/(|B_T|(F-1))\approx(1-|B_T|/F)/|B_T|$ for $\eta\ne0$ (uniformly random $B_T$ of fixed size), which gives $\mathbb E\,\mathrm{Cheb}_T\approx\mathrm{Hyb}_T$; in the random model $\sum_{\eta\ne0}|\hat\nu_U|^2\approx\sum_d(p^d-p^{d-1})2^{-n_{U,d}}$, whose top term is $(1-1/p)F2^{-n_U}$. ∎

**Values of $c_1(p,\rho)$** [computer-assisted, `logs/l2_lower_gate_shells.log`]: $c_1(13,\rho)=5.5,\,3.9,\,1.4,\,0.42,\,0.37,\,0.054,\,0.025,\,5.5\cdot10^{-4},\,1.5\cdot10^{-4},\,1.3\cdot10^{-4},\,4.1\cdot10^{-5},\,2.9\cdot10^{-5},\,2.2\cdot10^{-5}$ for $\rho=0,\dots,12$; $c_1(11,10)=1.4\cdot10^{-3}$; $c_1(7,6)=1.4\cdot10^{-2}$. So at $p=13$, even with all $13$ units present, $L^2\ge2.2\cdot10^{-5}\cdot13^M$, which exceeds $1$ from $M=5$ on; the Chebyshev criterion is dead for every window of depth $t\ge5-c$, in particular for every window relevant to $\mathrm{UC}(13)$.

### 3.3 Answer to "does the $L^2$ route have more room at $p=11,13$?"

**No** [proved by S5(b),(c)]. For the full-covering statement $\mathrm{Cov}$ the second-moment method is dominated by the $L^1$ method (b), and it fails outright at depth (c). What the $L^2$ quantity does control is the *fraction* of residues missed — a positive-measure statement — which is the right target for $\mu(K_p)>0$ (note §10.6 item 4), not for $\mathrm{Cov}$. The fibre hybrid (e) does not change this: its rigorous relaxation $\mathrm{CS}_T$ is useless at depth [proved, via the resonance $\eta=1$], and its exact value $\mathrm{Cheb}_T$ is, heuristically (random-set model), $\approx(1-1/p)F/R^*_T$, exponentially larger than the empirical threshold; on the certified windows of §3.4 the exact $\mathrm{Cheb}_T$ is $20$–$4\,800$ [computer-assisted].

A remark on why no second-moment statement can reproduce the empirical law: the observed miss count behaves like $\exp(-\gamma R_1)$ (note Conj. 10.25), a Poisson/coupon-collector law in the expected multiplicity; second moments only give $\le1/R^*$-type bounds on the miss *probability*. Turning the Poisson heuristic into a proof would need control of all moments, i.e. near-independence of the representation counts across residues — exactly what the resonance structure of $\hat E_p$ (§2.3) denies at low frequencies.

## 4. Computations: memory-reduced programs and depth-8 positions at $p=19$ [computer-assisted]

### 4.1 Why $p=19$ depth 8, and the accounting

With at most 3 cores and 4 GB per run, the fibre method of note §10.2 (programs A, B, C) is memory-bound by two bitsets of $p^{M-k}$ bits. For $p=19$, $t=8$, $H\in[39,57]$ one has $c=2$, $M=10$, and at $k=2$ a fibre is $19^8=1.70\cdot10^{10}$ bits $=2.0$ GB, so the published programs need $4.1$ GB. (Larger targets are out of reach: $p=23$ depth 8 needs $23^8$ bits $=9.8$ GB per bitset; $p=13$ depth 9 at $k=2$ needs $13^{10}$ bits $=17$ GB, and at $k=3$ a $2^{39}$-term low enumeration per position; $p=17$ depth 9 needs $17^9$ bits $=15$ GB.) ## 5. Gaps, and what remains

1. Theorems S1–S5 are elementary. Their value is in making exact what note §10.3 and §6 state informally; none of them is a step towards Q3 by itself. In particular S4 is a reformulation (like note Theorem 10.18), not progress: it moves the difficulty into the Fourier profile of the carry fibre.
2. The negative statements (S3′(ii), S5(b),(c)) are rigorous but narrow: they exclude fullness-only inductions and second-moment inductions for $\mathrm{Cov}$. They do not exclude an $L^1$-type argument, nor a non-Fourier argument, nor a different covering scheme.
3. The computations of §4 give one new unconditional (computer-assisted) bound, $f(1,19)\ge v_{19}((19^9-1)!)=17\,927\,094\,312$, by note Prop. 10.10. Every certificate it uses is checked by at least two independently written programs (three for $t\le6$ and the depth-7 classes $K''\le18$; exactly two, A′ and C′, for the 324 other depth-7 classes and the 18 depth-8 positions; §4.3(e)). A′ and C′ are modifications of programs A and C, validated against brute force at small sizes; there is no third program and no brute force at the $19^8$-bit size, and Proposition 10.10 itself is AI-refereed only (note §10.8).
4. The law tests of §4.3(f) are two further genuine out-of-sample tests of note Conj. 10.24 (one FAIL prediction confirmed by two programs with $2\,739\,774$ misses, one HOLD prediction confirmed at 18/18 positions by two programs) at a depth not previously tested; they do not make the law a theorem.
5. Floating-point diagnostics (§2.3 table, §3.4) are not certificates; the only exact computations are the identity checks (§1.3), the S3/S2(iv) check (§2.2), the brute-force validations, the exact small-depth coverage decisions of `chain_monotone_check.py`, and the coverage runs.
6. Two errors of the 2026-10-12 text were found in the 2026-10-20 re-check and corrected (revision note at the top; §2.5; §3.2(e)). A referee should assume that the remaining statements have been checked once more by the same kind of instance, not by a human.

**Most promising next step on this route.** Prove RMF-type decorrelation for the carry fibre at a single deep gate: show that the fibre measure $\mu_T$ of a window of length $p^{t-1}$ has $|\hat\mu_T(p^{t-d}\eta')|\le C\,(2/\pi)^{n_d'}$ on the shells $3\le d\le t$ with an explicit $C$, using S1's digit structure ($y_i=i!A_iB_i$, position entering at digit $t+v(i!)-\ell(i)$) and the $p$-automaticity of the unit parts (note Prop. 3.14). Theorem S4 then gives $\mathrm{Cov}(p,t,p^{t-1})$ and Theorem 3.12(c) gives Q3.
