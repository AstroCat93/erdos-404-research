5. **Literature bearing on the rate of $\delta_G(2)\to0$ [one small lemma proved; the rest heuristic].** The critical-BPRE survival asymptotics (Kozlov; Geiger–Kersting; Afanasyev–Geiger–Kersting–Vatutin) assume i.i.d. environments; there, in the zero-mean finite-variance case, they give $n^{-1/2}$. At $p=2$ the environment is the 2-adic odometer, and the walk is a digit-sum difference.
   - Lemma S0.1 [proved]: the probability that this walk stays $\ge0$ up to time $n$ is $\asymp(\log n)/n$, not $n^{-1/2}$.
   - Heuristic: the note's own published values satisfy $\delta_G(2)\cdot G/\log_2G\in[0.61,0.69]$ for all eleven $G$ from 8 to 254. Over the same range $\delta_G\sqrt G$ drifts from 0.69 down to 0.33. So the "$0.7/\sqrt G$" fit in note §5 and Remark 10.8.4 looks like a small-$G$ effect, and a $(\log G)/G$ law fits all the data [heuristic].
6. **One wording issue in note.md (for the lead).** Remark 10.8.2 says that for natural $x$ the walk $W_n$ "tends to $-\infty$ along record times". For natural $x$, $W_n=s_2(x+n)-s_2(x)\ge1-s_2(x)$ is bounded below. Divergence to $-\infty$ holds only on the generic set $\Omega$ (condition (G1)), so the sentence should be restricted to $\Omega$. The proof of Theorem 10.1 is unaffected.
7. **One citation detail (for the lead).**
   - erdosproblems.com prints Lin's memorandum as "(1960)" under the key `Li76`, and note.md copies 1960.
   - The `plasma-ai/erdos` wiki states that the Erdős–Graham monograph cites it as "[Lin (76)]", i.e. 1976, and that 1960 is a misprint.
   - We could not check the monograph ourselves.

## 2. Kitamura: the bound $f(594,2)\ge66126$, and the published exact values

### 2.1 How the bound was obtained (from Kitamura's own text)

Excerpts are saved verbatim in `data/round3/S0/kitamura_excerpts/p2_landscape_excerpts.md`, with commit and line numbers. The only edit is that the names of AI tools in Kitamura's disclosure are redacted, per project policy.
- **`witness_gallery.md` §9 (lines 195–213), "The champion without an exact value: a = 594".** "Certified by an increasing ladder of probes (selected rows; each row is an independently verified finite decision; state as of 2026-07-06)":
  ```
  g(594,2) >= 8192      55 s
  g(594,2) >= 16384     10 min
  g(594,2) >= 32768     61 min
  g(594,2) >= 65536     3.5 h      =>  f(594,2) >= 66126
  ```
- **`determining_exact_values.md` §5 (lines 229–237).** "f(594,2) >= 66126 (g(594,2) >= 65536, verified 2026-07-06) with deeper probes still running."
- **`README.md`, line 349.** "f(594,2) >= 66126 the current champion; no death found yet". Line 235: "every tracked spike except the champion 594 … has died".
- **Method.** Each rung is the finite decision "$g(a,2)\ge G$". By determinacy it depends only on $a \bmod 2^G$. Kitamura decides it by an exact forward dynamic programme over the reachable carry residues.
  - For deep rows Kitamura ships `scripts/p2_scaled_gate_search.py` (the "scaled gate DP"). It processes factorials in pairs: step 0 has options $\{0,a+1\}$; step $k\ge1$ has options $\{0,1,m,1+m\}$ with $m=a+2k+1$, consumes $e_k=v_2(a+2k+2)$ bits, and keeps every reachable state with no pruning.
  - The program raises an error if the state count exceeds `--max-states` (default $10^8$), so a "witness exists" answer is an exact decision, not a heuristic.
  - The doubling rungs $8192\to65536$ match its `bisect` mode, which doubles from `--start` (default cap 20000, so a raised cap must have been used) or four `one` runs.
  - "Independently verified" is not explained for $a=594$. For exact rows the README's house rule is that "both boundary decisions are re-run in a second, independently written implementation". The `compare` subcommand refers to `lab1/p2_gate_search.py`, which is **not** shipped.
- **What is published for $a=594$: nothing beyond the four lines above.**
  - There is no witness file. `data/` holds witnesses only for $a=34,1102,3456$.
  - There is no log, no metadata JSON, and no viewer entry. The viewer's `deep` rows are only 1102 and 3456.
  - The script used for this row is not named.
  - There has been no update since 2026-07-06/08, although deeper probes were said to be running.
- **Arithmetic check.** $594=2^9+2^6+2^4+2^1$, so $s_2(594)=4$ and $v_2(594!)=594-4=590$; $590+65536=66126$.

## 4. Literature bearing on the open routes (relevance assessed; URLs accessed 2026-10-13)

**(a) Rates of extinction for critical branching processes in random environment (BPRE).**
- V. I. Afanasyev, J. Geiger, G. Kersting, V. A. Vatutin, *Criticality for branching processes in random environment*, Ann. Probab. 33 (2005) 645–673, arXiv:math/0503657 (https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503657).
  - Re-read: Theorem 1.1, Corollary 1.2 and (1.3).
  - Setting: an **i.i.d.** environment whose associated random walk $S$ satisfies Spitzer's condition $\frac1n\sum_{m\le n}P(S_m>0)\to\rho\in(0,1)$, plus moment assumptions.
  - Result: $P(Z_n>0)\sim\theta\,P(\min(S_1,\dots,S_n)\ge0)\sim\theta n^{-(1-\rho)}l(n)$.
  - Mechanism: (1.3), $P(Z_n>0\mid\Pi)\le\exp\min_{m\le n}S_m$.
- M. V. Kozlov (1976). Its survival asymptotics $\asymp n^{-1/2}$, exact $\sim cn^{-1/2}$ in the linear-fractional case, and the extension by J. Geiger, G. Kersting (Theory Probab. Appl. 45, 2000/2001) to general offspring laws with a zero-mean, finite-variance walk are as summarised in the review E. Dyakonova, V. Vatutin, S. Sagitov, *Survival of branching processes in random environments*, arXiv:1110.6139 (https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.6139).
- Further results:
  - non-i.i.d. but **finite-state Markov** environments: I. Grama, R. Lauvergnat, E. Le Page, arXiv:1708.00233 (https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.00233);
  - the multi-type i.i.d. case, again $\propto n^{-1/2}$: E. Le Page, M. Peigné, C. Pham, Ann. Probab. 46(5) (2018) 2946–2972, doi:10.1214/17-AOP1243;
  - the weakly subcritical case: Afanasyev, Böinghoff, Kersting, Vatutin, J. Theoret. Probab. 25 (2012) 703–732, arXiv:1001.1672;
  - slightly supercritical i.i.d. environments (Haldane asymptotics): F. Boenkost, G. Kersting, arXiv:2109.10684.
- **Bearing.** None of these covers our environment. At $p=2$ the environment is the 2-adic **odometer**: deterministic given $x$, stationary under Haar measure, neither i.i.d. nor finite-state Markov. Its associated walk is a digit-sum difference (§5). We found no BPRE survival-rate result for odometer or Toeplitz environments. The $n^{-1/2}$ law must therefore not be transferred to $\delta_G(2)$; Lemma S0.1 and Remark S0.2 below show why.

**(b) Positive measure via second moments; correlated offspring.**
- Classical a.s.-survival criteria for supercritical BPRE in stationary ergodic environments go back to Athreya–Karlin (1971), already cited in the note.
- The moment method for percolation on trees with dependent (k-independent) percolation: P. Mathieu, C. Temmel, *K-independent percolation on trees*, SPA 122 (2012) 1129–1153, arXiv:1103.1291 (https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.1291). It extends Lyons' second-moment bounds when the dependence decays with the depth of the confluent. This is the closest template we found for an $L^2$ bound on the odd-$p$ analogue of the §10.1 martingale (note §10.6 item 4), where carries are correlated.
- We found **no** result on positive Haar measure of zero sets or of "0 lies in the subsum set" for random $p$-adic series.

**(c) Subset sums and achievement sets in $\mathbb Z_p$; digits of Morita's $\Gamma_p$.**
- K. G. Hare, T. Vávra, *Self-similar sets and self-similar measures in the p-adics*, arXiv:2307.07375 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07375). This is the only $p$-adic analogue of Bernoulli-convolution or achievement-set theory we found. It covers self-similar sets and measures, not subsums of non-geometric series.
- The real theory of achievement sets continues, for example arXiv:2512.17761 and 2412.08768, but there is no $p$-adic analogue. This confirms the note's §2 statement.
- For Morita's $\Gamma_p$ we found only the standard theory (local analyticity, functional equation, Gross–Koblitz) and distribution formulas for $p$-adic log-Gamma (arXiv:1702.04200). ## 5. Lemma S0.1 [proved] and the environment walk at $p=2$

**Setting.** For $y\in\mathbb Z_2$ and an integer $k\ge1$, let $c(y,k)\in\{0,1,\dots\}\cup\{\infty\}$ be the number of carries in the binary addition $y+k$. Put $D(y,k):=s_2(k)-c(y,k)$.
- $c(y,k)=\infty$ only if the carry runs through infinitely many 1-digits, i.e. only if $y$ is a negative integer. That set is countable and Haar-null; we exclude it.
- For $y\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, Kummer's carry count $c(y,k)=s_2(y)+s_2(k)-s_2(y+k)$ gives $D(y,k)=s_2(y+k)-s_2(y)$.
- With $x=y$, this is the walk of note Remark 10.8.2: $W_n=n-V_n$ in general, and $W_n=s(n)-c(x,n)$ for natural $x$. The pair-gate walk below is this walk sampled at even times, $s_2(a+2n+2)-s_2(a)=D(a,2n+2)=D(a/2,n+1)$, shifted by $-1$.

**Identity (environment of Kitamura's pair gates).** Let $a$ be even and $n\ge0$. The pair-gate DP has 2 options at step 0 and 4 at each step $k\ge1$, and consumes $e_k=v_2(a+2k+2)$ bits at step $k$. Then
$$\sum_{k=0}^{n}\big(\log_2\#\mathrm{options}_k-e_k\big)=s_2(a+2n+2)-s_2(a)-1 .$$

*Proof.* The left side is $1+2n-\sum_{k=0}^{n}v_2(a+2k+2)$. Put $b=a/2$. Then
$$\sum_{k=0}^{n}v_2\big(2(b+k+1)\big)=(n+1)+v_2\Big(\frac{(b+n+1)!}{b!}\Big)=(n+1)+(b+n+1-s_2(b+n+1))-(b-s_2(b))$$
by Legendre's formula $v_2(m!)=m-s_2(m)$. The left side is therefore $1+2n-(2n+2)+s_2(b+n+1)-s_2(b)$, and $s_2(2m)=s_2(m)$ gives the claim. ∎

(Checked numerically for all even $a<3000$ and $n<400$; 0 mismatches; `env_walk_check.log`.) This is the mean-field count $2^{s_2(N)-s_2(a)-1}$ of note §5 (H1), restated as the associated walk of a BPRE.

**Lemma S0.1.** Let $y$ be Haar-distributed on $\mathbb Z_2$, let $n\ge1$, $m=\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor$, and $E_n=\{D(y,k)\ge0\text{ for all }1\le k\le n\}$. Then
$$\frac{m+2}{2^{m+2}}\le P(E_n)\le\frac{m+2}{2^{m+1}},\qquad\text{so}\qquad\frac{\log_2n+1}{4n}<P(E_n)\le\frac{\log_2n+2}{n}.$$

*Proof.* Discard the null set of negative integers and $y=0$. Let $j=v_2(y)$ and write $y=\sum_iy_i2^i$ with $y_i\in\{0,1\}$.

*Upper bound.* Suppose $E_n$ holds and $j<m$. We show by induction on $i=j+1,\dots,m$ that $y_i=0$.
- Assume $y_{j+1}=\dots=y_{i-1}=0$; this is vacuous for $i=j+1$. Then $y\bmod2^i=2^j$.
- Put $k=2^i-2^j$. Then $1\le k<2^i\le2^m\le n$.
- In the addition $y+k$, positions $j,\dots,i-1$ hold $1+1$ and then $0+1+\text{carry}$, which gives $i-j$ carries and a carry into position $i$. That carry then runs through the $t\ge0$ consecutive 1-digits $y_i=y_{i+1}=\dots=y_{i+t-1}=1$ and stops at the next 0-digit, which exists because $y$ is not a negative integer.
- So $c(y,k)=i-j+t$, $s_2(k)=i-j$ and $D(y,k)=-t$.
- $E_n$ forces $t=0$, i.e. $y_i=0$.

Hence $E_n\cap\{j<m\}\subseteq\bigcup_{j=0}^{m-1}\{v_2(y)=j,\ y_{j+1}=\dots=y_m=0\}$. Each event in the union has probability $2^{-(j+1)}2^{-(m-j)}=2^{-(m+1)}$. Adding $P(v_2(y)\ge m)=2^{-m}$ gives
$$P(E_n)\le m2^{-(m+1)}+2^{-m}=(m+2)2^{-(m+1)}.$$

*Lower bound.* Let $0\le j\le m+1$, and suppose $v_2(y)=j$ and $y_{j+1}=\dots=y_{m+1}=0$. Then $y=2^j+2^{m+2}w$ with $w\in\mathbb Z_2$.
- For $1\le k\le n$ we have $2^j+k\le2^{m+1}+n<2^{m+2}$, because $n<2^{m+1}$. So no carry leaves position $m+1$, and $c(y,k)=c(2^j,k)$.
- Hence $D(y,k)=s_2(2^j+k)-s_2(2^j)=s_2(2^j+k)-1\ge0$, and $E_n$ holds.
- These $m+2$ events (one for each $j$) are disjoint, and each has probability $2^{-(j+1)}2^{-(m+1-j)}=2^{-(m+2)}$.

The displayed consequences follow from $2^m\le n<2^{m+1}$. ∎

*Numerical check* (exact enumeration of $y\bmod2^{20}$; residues where a carry could leave the window are counted separately, which gives rigorous enclosures):
- every enclosure for $n=1,2,4,\dots,1024$ lies within the lemma's bounds;
- for example $P(E_{256})\in[0.01855,0.01880]$, inside $[0.00977,0.01953]$;
- the data suggest $P(E_{2^m})=(2m+3)/2^{m+2}$ exactly (not proved).

Command: `nice -n 10 python3 -I code/round3/S0/env_walk_check.py 20 1024`. Log: `data/round3/S0/env_walk_check.log`; wall time 59 s.

**Remark S0.2 [heuristic; not a result]: the rate of $\delta_G(2)$.**
- In i.i.d. environments, AGKV Theorem 1.1 says survival to time $n$ costs $\theta\,P(\min_{k\le n}S_k\ge0)$. For a zero-mean, finite-variance walk this is $\asymp n^{-1/2}$.
- In the odometer environment the analogous probability is $\asymp(\log n)/n$ by Lemma S0.1. The depth $G$ in bits and the number $n$ of factorials processed are comparable ($V_n=n+O(\log n)$ on $\Omega$; note Lemma 10.2(a),(b) and (G2)), so $(\log n)/n$ and $(\log G)/G$ are interchangeable here. The same argument suggests $P(\min\ge-x)\asymp(\log n)^{x+1}/n$ for fixed $x$; this is not proved.
- So *if* an AGKV-type relation held for the 2-adic carry process, $\delta_G(2)$ would decay like $(\log G)^{c}/G$, not like $G^{-1/2}$. The relation is unproved here: offspring are not independent (carry residues merge), and the environment is not i.i.d.
- The note's published values (exact $\delta_G$ for $G\le32$ from §4.7; Monte Carlo with $4\cdot10^6$ samples for $G=64,128,254$ from §5) fit this law. The table is arithmetic on published numbers only:

| $G$ | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 | 24 | 28 | 29 | 32 | 64 | 128 | 254 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\delta_G(2)$ | .2422 | .1958 | .1517 | .1417 | .1292 | .1164 | .1134 | .1016 | .0644 | .0379 | .0208 |
| $\delta_G\sqrt G$ | .685 | .678 | .607 | .634 | .633 | .616 | .611 | .575 | .515 | .429 | .331 |
| $\delta_G\,G/\log_2G$ | .646 | .655 | .607 | .656 | .676 | .678 | .677 | .650 | .687 | .693 | .661 |
| $\delta_G\,G$ | 1.94 | 2.35 | 2.43 | 2.83 | 3.10 | 3.26 | 3.29 | 3.25 | 4.12 | 4.85 | 5.28 |

- $\delta_G\,G/\log_2G$ stays in $[0.607,0.693]$ over a 30-fold range of $G$. $\delta_G\sqrt G$ halves over the same range, and $\delta_G G$ nearly triples.
- The local exponents of the data (0.63 from $G=8$ to 32; 0.82 from 64 to 254) match those of $(\log G)/G$, namely $1-1/\ln G$: 0.64 at $G=16$ and 0.79 at $G=128$.
- **Consequence for the note.** The fit "$\delta_G(2)\approx0.7/\sqrt G$" (§5; Remark 10.8.4) looks like a small-$G$ artifact. Kitamura's "$\sim11.5/G$" has the same exponent as this heuristic, but its constant does not match our $\delta_G$ ($11.5/254=0.045$ against 0.0208). Kitamura's normalisation is not stated, so the two can only be compared in exponent.
- **Integers.** For even $a\ge2$ the walk is bounded below: $D(a/2,\cdot)\ge1-s_2(a/2)$, i.e. $s_2(a+2n+2)-s_2(a)-1\ge-s_2(a)$. So the first-moment mechanism (AGKV (1.3)) can never force extinction at a natural number. For $a=594$ the quenched mean never drops below $2^{-4}$. This is consistent with Theorem 10.1 saying nothing about integers, and it says nothing either way about $a=594$.
